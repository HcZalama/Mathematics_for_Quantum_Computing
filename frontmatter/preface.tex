\markboth{Preface}{Preface}

This book grew out of two courses on the mathematics of quantum computing, taught to undergraduate students who want to understand quantum
computers and not only to run programs on them. Quantum mechanics is, mathematically, linear algebra in a particular dress, and a quantum
computer is a machine that manipulates one vector in that dress. If the students master the dress, the rest of the programme (circuits, algorithms,
error correction) becomes a matter of using it, so this book spends its pages on the dress.

\paragraph{What it tries to do.}
There are excellent references for the subject, and the standard one~\cite{nielsenchuang2010} is the model for much of what follows. This book differs in emphasis. It is meant to be \emph{read}
by someone meeting these ideas for the first time, so it explains before it states, it prefers one worked example with all the numbers to three paragraphs of generalities, and it keeps its length under control.
Every concept that matters is followed by a worked example, and every section ends with a graded list of exercises, so that the reader always has something to try.

\paragraph{Who it is for.}
Readers with a first course in calculus and linear algebra, and some curiosity about physics. No quantum mechanics is assumed. The second part of the book reviews, and where necessary builds, every piece of mathematics that it uses.

\paragraph{How to read it.}
Read with a pencil. The worked examples are the heart of the book: cover the solution, try the example, and compare. The exercises are marked with one, two or three stars according to difficulty. A one-star exercise should take a few minutes and checks that you
have understood the definition; a three-star exercise may need an idea, and it is fine to leave it for later. The Introduction explains the visual conventions of the book.

\paragraph{About the use of AI in this book.}
This book was written with the assistance of an AI model (Claude, by Anthropic). The author decided the scope of the book, the order in which ideas are introduced, the level of difficulty, the examples and exercises to include and the connections between topics, working from his own lecture slides, his collection of exercises and standard references.
The AI was used as a writing and typesetting assistant: drafting text from those materials, generating figures in the book's typesetting system, and checking calculations. Numerical results were checked by computer where this was possible. The author reviewed the content and is responsible for it, including any errors that remain.
Corrections and suggestions from students and colleagues are welcome and will be incorporated into future editions.
